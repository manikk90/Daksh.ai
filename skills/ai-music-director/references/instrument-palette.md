# Instrument palette

Choose roles, not an instrument shopping list. Start with one lead, harmonic/drone support, a rhythm family and limited replies. Inspect resulting timbre; models may substitute instruments. Cultural associations are context-sensitive.

| Instrument/family | Role and articulation | Starting use/caution |
|---|---|---|
| Bansuri | Breathy lead, birdlike runs, call-response | Krishna warmth, innocence, curiosity; leave breath space |
| Shehnai | Reedy ceremonial line or short comic retort | Ritual/folk color; avoid accidental wedding mood in grief |
| Algoza | Interlocking breath-driven folk pulse | North Indian folk motion where appropriate |
| Clarinet | Nimble woody runs, comic held note | Vintage comedy and acoustic replies |
| Oboe/bassoon | Plaintive reed / low dry articulation | Vulnerability or wit with a specified role |
| Mouth organ | Small warm melodic answer | Childhood, travel, nostalgic adventure |
| Sarangi | Bowed slides, sustained expressive phrase | Yearning/grief; do not overplay under dialogue |
| Violin/viola | Solo legato, tremolo, counterline | Lullaby, grief, tension; specify register/attack |
| Cello/double bass | Low lament / pizzicato pulse | Emotional foundation or tiptoe comedy |
| String ensemble | Divisi swell, ostinato, restrained pad | Scale through contrast, not a constant wall |
| Veena | Resonant plucks, ornamental reply | Tender devotion; retain when explicitly requested |
| Sitar/surbahar | Bright clipped plucks / deep resonance | Sparkle or meditative weight with sparse phrasing |
| Sarod | Rounded plucked melody | Reflective/grave lead; distinguish from sarangi |
| Santoor | Hammered sparkle and repeated figures | Luminous release; avoid dense high-register masking |
| Mandolin | Precise picking or tremolo | Folk adventure and comedy replies |
| Ektara | Sparse single-string pulse | Folk devotional simplicity |
| Tanpura | Sustained resonant drone | Melodic grounding, not chord strumming |
| Harmonium | Reed sustain, short chord replies | Bhajan/folk warmth; limit chords for raga-led work |
| Piano/celesta | Felt intimacy / delicate bell notes | Contemporary emotion or wonder; avoid generic fallback |
| Dholak | Rounded alternating hand groove | Playful domestic energy and folk movement |
| Tabla | Articulated bols, conversational pulse | Rhythmic detail without clutter |
| Pakhawaj | Weighty resonant strokes | Ceremonial gravity and epic development |
| Nagara/dhol | Broad impact / forward power | Action/procession; protect dialogue/SFX |
| Duggi/matka/ghatam | Small hand pulse / pot resonance | Light acoustic bounce; select idiom deliberately |
| Khanjira | Crisp hand-drum color | Playfulness; does not define every region |
| Khartal/manjira | Wooden clacks / small cymbal pulse | Folk/devotional motion; control brightness |
| Claps/ankle bells | Human groove / fine sparkle | Song/dance where requested; do not invent dancers |
| Temple bell/chime/shankh | Sparse resonance / ceremonial accent | Divine punctuation; distinguish from source SFX |
| Marimba/xylophone/woodblocks | Short pitched/wooden gestures | Tiptoe comedy/curiosity; avoid cartooning all child scenes |
| Muted trumpet/trombone/tuba | Brass replies / low comic weight | Vintage gags or controlled ensemble peak |
| Horn/low brass | Warm breadth / powerful foundation | Mythic scale saved for dramatic turn |
| Brushed snare/frame drum | Soft regular movement / earthy pulse | Travel, montage or restrained drive |
| Synth bass/pads/pulses | Designed low end and evolving texture | Hybrid/contemporary when allowed |
| Voice/humming/choir | Human melodic/harmonic layer | Only if authorized; all forbidden in strict instrumental |

## Ensemble recipes

- Laddu playful: bansuri; dholak/matka; khartal; mandolin replies; light harmonium. Young-girl singing only for a song request.
- Laddu intimacy: sparse veena/tanpura; flute replies; rare bell; little percussion under speech.
- Vintage comedy alternate: clarinet motif, pizzicato bass, woodblock rests and mandolin; evolve rhythm and phrase design.
- Hanuman tragedy: sarangi/violin, low cello, tanpura and exposed veena; restrained pakhawaj, unresolved tail.
- Vayu rage: low-string ostinato, pakhawaj/nagara, dark brass and fragmented grief motif.
- Miracle: familiar flute motif grows through strings/santoor/veena and one ceremonial accent, then strips back.

## Raga and rhythm

Name raga/tala only when useful. Distinguish strict raga composition from loose inspiration. For strict work establish and verify pitch/phrase grammar from a reliable reference; Western chord changes can conflict. A text tag cannot establish performed raga accuracy.

A Dadra-like lilt, six-beat cycle and written 6/8 are not automatically identical. Clarify the pulse when it affects an existing melody/cut. Keep culturally coherent phrasing without treating instruments as exclusively belonging to a single region.
