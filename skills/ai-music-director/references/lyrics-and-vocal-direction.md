# Lyrics, prosody and vocal direction

## Write to a relationship and melody

Select an adaptable structure from `assets/lyric-patterns.json` and phrase-performance guidance from `references/vocal-micro-expression.md` for new writing. Use the requested form first; a pattern does not authorize changing a locked hook. Save demonstrated writing improvements with a version and target/boundary case; do not copy stock verses from an earlier rejected draft.

1. Establish speaker, addressee, relationship, tense, story moment, audience and register. A child-friend, motherly caregiver and grateful adult have different diction.
2. Resolve rewrite depth: rhyme repair, verse improvement, locked-hook expansion, full replacement, adaptation or new song. Preserve approved words. After repeated rejected drafts create fresh lexical and structural material.
3. Choose an engine: daily conversation, playful service, complaint answered by trust, remembered rescue or emotional change. Each verse needs a new detail/function rather than synonyms for the chorus.
4. Shape a memorable short hook and its emotional promise. Use the supplied rhythm; do not offer ten options when the user requested a finished lyric.
5. Fit the form: title theme, full song, lullaby, montage or reprise. Do not default to three long verses/two bridges. Estimate timing from phrases/tempo/repeats and label unmeasured duration.
6. Speak/tap the intended accents and breath groups. Compare phrase stress, not merely word counts. Keep lyrics singable before expanding orchestration.
7. Place instrumental answers, breaths and arrangement lifts where the words permit them.

## Hindi rhyme and singability

- Use rhyme families that serve meaning, e.g. गोपाल/कमाल/ख्याल/सँभाल. Distinguish approximate sonic links from exact rhyme. Avoid forcing every verse into the same ending.
- Preserve complete ordinary phrases; do not truncate उजाला to उजाल or insert an awkward kinship word solely for rhyme. Use लला/लाला deliberately and consistently with the chosen register.
- Maintain grammatical person/honorific agreement: तू/तुम/आप and verb forms. Distinguish imagined quoted replies from the singer's own voice.
- Keep natural stress, vowel flow and consonant articulation. Long vowels/name syllables may support melodic holds; dense clusters need time.
- Written characters, linguistic syllables and sung matra duration differ. Schwa deletion, dialect, combining marks and melisma prevent simple counting from proving meter. Use spoken/sung review; do not invent exact counts.
- Make childlike delivery clear and conversational; do not require baby talk, exaggerated nasal tone or adult belting.
- Replace generic lists of श्रद्धा/आशा/विश्वास with concrete actions and personal observations where appropriate. Keep devotion tender; playful service need not become disrespectful comedy.

## Narrative truth

An established idol relationship starts in ongoing daily life. Post-miracle thanks can describe received help; a pre-miracle song cannot assert rescue already happened. Keep generic title-singer POV separate from Naina. For her perspective use sound, touch, memory and inner feeling as appropriate; do not add a school routine, physical visual perception or cure without source authority.

The brass-versus-faith metaphor is optional, not a mandatory bridge. An inner-vision metaphor needs artistic intent; do not quietly treat it as eyesight. Do not reuse the same metaphor to fill every rewrite.

| Song mode | Language behavior | Vocal/arrangement direction |
|---|---|---|
| Playful child-devotional theme | Short concrete lines, return hook, call-response | Light attack, clear words, smiling conversational phrasing, flute replies |
| Daily seva | Specific care, intimate second person | Warm restrained voice and breathing room |
| Gratitude | Hardship → received help → thanks | Inward opening, rising relief, earned refrain |
| Lullaby | Soft vowels, rocking repetition, quiet closure | Narrow comfortable range, gentle dynamics |
| Bhajan/kirtan | Accessible responsive return when requested | Distinct lead/group roles; no group voices if forbidden |
| Narrative/ad/promo | Clear progression and timed hook | Intelligibility, short phrases and usable edit points |

These are artistic directions, not guaranteed controls on every platform.

## Regional adaptations

For Marathi, Haryanvi, Rajasthani, Bengali, Bhojpuri and Gujarati:
1. Lock semantic beat, hook function, deity/name pronunciation, contour and available phrase duration.
2. Adapt idiomatic register/rhyme; do not force Hindi word order or treat neighboring dialects as interchangeable.
3. Map stresses, holds, breaths and consonant density to each phrase; change wording or propose melodic adjustment if necessary.
4. Keep native-script master lyrics. Put optional transliteration/phonetic workarounds in a separate singer/platform guide, not the display lyrics.
5. Plan competent native review of meaning, dialect and sung pronunciation; mark pending if unavailable.
6. Retain approved instrumental/stems where fit/access permit. Do not regenerate a new theme merely because the vocal language changed. Recheck entries and final phrase timing.

Speech dubbing and sung performance are different. Speech audio tags or a saved speech voice do not establish singing control or an identical singing voice.

## Delivery and checks

For lyrics plus music, separate Styles and Lyrics fields. Use supported section tags; keep explanation outside copy blocks. Style describes delivery/arrangement rather than the whole screenplay.

Check requested novelty, hook locks, POV/tense, agreement, idiom, rhyme, breath/stress, verse development, ending and story truth. Call this text-checked; melody/singer testing remains pending until an actual sung take is reviewed.
