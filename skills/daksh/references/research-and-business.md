# Research reuse and business analysis

Search matching findings, source pointers and prior experiments first. Resolve original artifacts for load-bearing claims. Old Seedance/composition/harness/music/dubbing research can be useful while model claims, media validation or installation remain stale/pending.

Convert evidence into problem → verified finding → tool/interface/date → proposed action → test → expected value → limitation. Prioritize official docs, original papers/repos and real customer evidence. Separate event/publication/retrieval dates. Label old baseline guides; do not report them as new releases. Verify current access, pricing, model parameters and competition before relying on them.

For business work identify buyer, painful job, present workaround, offer, differentiation, willingness-to-pay hypothesis, acquisition path, unit-economic assumptions, smallest MVP, risks and a cheap falsifiable test. Choose build/test/research/park/kill with reasons. A product announcement is not validated demand. Leave unknown market, cost, conversion and latency values null; use Data for supported quantitative work.

Explore across industries, using Manik's Context Engineering/production strengths without forcing every idea into his shows. Advance hypothesis → research → experiment_ready → testing → validated only with matching evidence. Track previous suggestions/outcomes so a weekly review advances work instead of recycling headlines.

Default outputs: recommendation plus fit/limitations/test for tools; decision plus buyer/offer/MVP/demand test for ideas; at most three useful findings and one progressed opportunity for weekly synthesis. Do not duplicate the daily five-news or weekly six-idea Slack task.

Use existing evidence/free local checks first. Do not spend generation credits, subscribe, publish or contact prospects merely for maintenance research unless separately authorized. Distinguish executed tests from recommendations and source claims from measured benefits.
