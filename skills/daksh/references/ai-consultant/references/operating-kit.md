# First paid pilot

**Positioning:** I help small brand teams and ad agencies turn one product brief into a production-ready ad storyboard, before they commit to shooting or generating a full spot.

**Offer:** 30-Second Ad Storyboard Sprint. Pilot price ₹5,000 (a test, not validated market pricing). One concept, six frames, estimated timing and copy-ready prompts, one consolidated revision. Proposed delivery: 48 hours after the agreed brief, usable access and agreed deposit. No final video, media buying, additional concepts, paid credits or outside asset rights. Proposed payment: ₹2,500 upfront and ₹2,500 on delivery. Agree milestones and any taxes or fees before booking. Target four labor hours: ₹1,250/hour gross at four hours before expenses, ₹625/hour at eight. Reprice or narrow scope if the effort consistently exceeds target.

## Rights-clean portfolio demo

**Spec example: “Raah,” a fictional steel bottle.** Hook: the commute hits pause; hydration doesn't. No real logos, celebrity likenesses, licensed tracks or employer materials. Label “Spec concept · fictional brand · no client results.”

Portfolio page: brief → six frames → three decisions → one revision or QC example → what a buyer receives. Show planning and continuity, not made-up outcome metrics. Start with one strong demo. Create a public-safe copy only if and when authorized; keep proposals/client files separate.

## Intake and scope

Ask: What must the audience do? Who are they? What is the product and one verifiable claim? Where will this play and in which ratio/language? What assets or reference rights are cleared? Who approves, when is feedback due, and what is the deadline and budget?

Quote block: goal | deliverables and file format | excluded work | rights/AI disclosure | required inputs | start date/turnaround | one feedback round and deadline | acceptance criteria | proposed fees/milestones | what triggers a change order. Record approval and version. Never start a costly or externally committed step on an ambiguous brief.

## Safe delivery and feedback

- Default for private material: Google Drive on an authorized account, Restricted, verified named address, Viewer. Use expiry if available; otherwise agree a revoke date. Confirm folder inheritance and recipient/role on the actual file. No default public link, no raw sources, no credential sharing. Watermark private review copies if useful; it is not a screenshot barrier.
- Export a clean review file; inspect comments, hidden layers, document properties, embedded links, faces/music and third-party rights. Open the final artifact before sharing and keep an access record: artifact/version, recipient, role, approval and revoke date.
- Get authorization for recipient, content and permissions; then send the exact approved artifact, verify the result, and report what was shared. Ask for a testimonial or publish the case study only with permissions.

## Seven-day test

Day 1: offer and sample brief. Days 2–3: finish one rights-clean demo and check the handoff. Day 4: qualify ten buyers privately. Days 5–7: prepare personalized pitches and request approval before sending. Track buyer, fit, source, stage, response, appointment, quote, paid, hours, cash costs, next step and due date. A useful early signal is one paid pilot, not likes or views. After ten qualified pitches with no meaningful replies, revise buyer, hook or offer. If a pilot is profitable and useful, repeat once before scaling.

## 30-second Marketer-to-Director pilot

For the existing fictional, rights-clean bottle example: audience city commuters, problem rummaging for a bottle, promise easy access demonstrated by a visible side-pocket retrieval, offer a concept preview (not product availability), one CTA. Verify no unsupported thermal, safety or leakproof claim.

- 0–3s: first-frame hook. A: bottle clicks into the accessible side pocket. B: same opening and copy timing, but bag-rummage visual first; only the opening visual changes.
- 3–8s: establish the buyer's everyday friction and the commute.
- 8–20s: two proof frames for retrieval and use, matching cap/hand/pocket continuity.
- 20–26s: payoff, bag and product ready for the next commute.
- 26–30s: clear CTA and hero hold; no fabricated testimonial or performance stat.

Handoff: six frames total; use the same body and CTA for both options, preserve ratio and captions, confirm every promise shown can be supported. Review the first frame and CTA with sound off. Before any approved run, record early hold and completion separately from qualified inquiries; a no-spend review can only test comprehension, not sales lift.
