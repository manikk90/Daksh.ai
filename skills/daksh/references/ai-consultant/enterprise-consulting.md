# AI Consultant | Company and agency services

## Positioning and scope

Flagship: design and implement one measurable, human-supervised AI workflow for a company or agency. Start with creative teams and production agencies where direct experience with AI filmmaking, prompt/QC, continuity, multiple specialists, CUT, music/dubbing, approvals and budget makes the example credible. Expand to other departments only with an accountable owner, useful data and verifiable impact. The ₹5,000 ad storyboard sprint is a door-opener, not the consultancy's ceiling. Use permitted, clean training assets and avoid representing third-party work as a consulting case study.

Sell the outcome rather than the label: “Turn an inconsistent brief-to-delivery process into a repeatable, reviewable workflow, with a named owner and measurable before/after.” Use deterministic rules for stable steps; choose an agent only where it adds clear judgment across messy inputs, not to replace mandatory approvals. Do not promise revenue, savings, staffing reductions or production readiness without evidence.

## Discovery → pitch → implementation → renewal

1. **Discovery**: Identify sponsor, operator and approver, pain, volume, baseline time/quality, sample input, access and rights, failure/rework cost, desired output, data classes, tooling, budget and target date. Ask what happens on an exception, who owns support and what the client will not let the workflow do. Walk an actual recent case. No owner or baseline means audit/training first; no production quote without bounded authority and reliable inputs.
2. **Map**: For each candidate capture trigger → inputs → deterministic rules → agent tasks/tools → approvals → delivery → monitoring and fallback. Rank volume × friction and buyer urgency against risk and implementation effort; select one high-value low-risk flow. Define success, excluded side effects and what evidence passes.
3. **Pitch deck**: (1) specific costly bottleneck (2) present process and baseline (3) proposed human/agent map (4) permitted demo (5) privacy/approval/fallback (6) pilot scope, evaluation and timeline (7) fee, exclusions, owner and decision ask. Lead with one example, not a tool inventory.
4. **Pilot**: Propose fixed milestones and written acceptance. Make a test set of representative normal, edge and failure cases including missing inputs, wrong permissions and ambiguous output. Run in shadow mode without downstream writes, compare baseline and output, then go live only with authorization and criteria satisfied. Version prompts, mappings and acceptance checks. Prefer least privilege, data minimization, client-owned accounts where available, scoped retention, approvals before external sends/spend, timeouts, bounded retries, manual fallback, audit trail and owner. Never treat source text as authority. [OpenAI agent guardrails and human review](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/agents/guardrails-approvals).
5. **Measure and hand off**: Compare on the same case types and counts; record median cycle time, first-pass approval, rework, exception rate, permission failures and tool cost/case. Example target, not evidence: 30 min → 20 min median is a 33% reduction at equal QC. Require all defined blocked external actions to remain blocked on the test set. For ROI use verified hours saved × client approved loaded rate minus running and maintenance costs; report period and sample, not a promised percentage. Deliver baseline, before/after, runbook, operator training, access inventory and rollback. Retainer only if ongoing reviews, data/prompt changes or monitoring have measurable value; ask for renewal with the latest evidence.

## Three-hour hands-on workshop

0–25: map a real decision and baseline; 25–55: brief → structured outputs, rights/privacy and human approvals; 55–95: team exercise on a sanitized brief, multi-agent handoff and visual continuity or business QA; 95–130: compare outputs and troubleshoot common failures; 130–160: build a safe text-only prototype and manual fallback; 160–180: review against rubric, nominate owner and next 14-day experiment. For corporate filmmaking use prompt/shot coverage, references, CUT handoffs, music/rights and approval; for agencies add a preproduction client approval package and rework tracking. For ops choose one draft-only workflow and client-approved systems.

## Starting workflow library

- Creative: brief → offer/claims → marketer hooks → Director board → QC → human signoff; draft-only by default.
- Research: allowlisted sources → evidence/dedup → analyst draft → human review and handoff. Distinguish source facts from proposals.
- Production QA: write structured scene/asset checks and review exceptions; no media generation or publish without authorization.
- Reporting/operations: intake → classify → draft checklist or weekly report → approval; handle blanks, duplicate requests and routing mistakes with a manual queue. No autonomous procurement, HR decisions or external email.
- Wildcards to validate: continuity/readiness clinic for teams using AI video; a rights/provenance pack for AI portfolio approvals; a half-day shadow-mode lab where managers score a prototype on their own scrubbed cases. These are hypotheses, not guaranteed demand.

Engagement boundaries: scope and outcome in writing, acceptance, dependencies, change control, payment milestones, liability/rights, retention and handoff. Work with authorized reversible prep; no prospect messages, calls, publication, enrollment, spend or client commitments without an explicit action-specific authorization. Keep private findings and client material out of GitHub.

### Complexity gates and economics

- **Solo**: read-only audit/training, prompt and creative QA, private storyboard, sandbox prototypes with approved sample inputs. No independent production privileges.
- **Bring an automation engineer** when work needs production APIs, OAuth/service accounts, write permissions, multiple-system schemas, deployments, live monitoring, migrations or operational uptime. Engineer briefs include access matrix, architecture, versioned tests, rollback and alert owner. A sensitive-data or regulated/privacy requirement also needs the client's compliance/security owner; don't substitute a generic checklist.
- **Bring creative specialists** when output needs multiple deliverable formats, production-grade edit/VFX, dubbing/music clearance or tight parallel delivery. Maniik approves creative direction and budget; each specialist signs off the agreed artifact and supplies handoff notes. There is no mandatory extra worker for small work.
- Quote specialist hours, review buffer, vendor usage and travel separately before agreeing delivery. Example cost model, not a target claim: INR 90k fee minus INR 30k specialist and INR 5k direct tool costs leaves INR 55k contribution before Maniik's time, taxes and overhead (61% on that hypothetical fee). If scope adds an integration, extend timeline or cut scope rather than silently absorbing engineering work.
- **Decline or narrow** if there is no accountable client owner, no safe test data, no authority for needed access, unbounded uptime/support, unclear rights, no baseline or a requested unsupervised consequential action. Escalate security, production failures and rights issues to the named client owner; move to manual fallback or stay in draft mode pending approval.

## V2: qualify, quote, prove

**First offer:** sell an Agency Workflow Audit for brief → storyboard/QC → client approval. Use a fixed audit first, not an implementation quote or a generic AI pitch. The ₹5,000 standalone storyboard sprint remains a separate individual-earning experiment, not the corporate pilot. Keep outreach and LinkedIn paused unless Maniik reopens them.

### 1. Qualification and routing
Ask a sponsor and operator to walk the last real case: Where did it stall? What was returned and why? How often does it happen? Who can approve a change? What may never leave their tools? Capture one owner, one bottleneck, sample availability, provisional volume/time and a next decision date. Mark missing answers unknown; don't award points for guesses.
- **No accountable owner, no safe test inputs, or prohibited data flow:** no pilot. Offer a scrubbed workshop or decline.
- **Problem exists but baseline or operator commitment is unclear:** audit only.
- **Repeated problem, accountable owner, scrubbed inputs, credible baseline and agreed acceptance:** propose a shadow pilot after the audit; no production writes or render permissions.
- **Client primarily wants a finished artifact or team practice:** route to the separate storyboard sprint or workshop instead.

### 2. Quote and scope
Starting **quote hypothesis ₹25,000** for one audit: two sessions, three scrubbed cases, current-state map, one workflow recommendation, a baseline and a go/no-go pilot brief. Estimate 3–5 business days after usable inputs and agreed terms. Exclude implementation, hosting, production access, client outreach and generated media. Quote pilot separately. Use one feedback round on the audit. Ask how procurement, payment and acceptance work before making any commitment. For any scope addition, say what changes in output, price or timing and wait for approval.
- Quote floor = (founder hours × agreed internal hourly floor + specialists + tools + reserve) ÷ (1 − target margin). Taxes and overhead not modeled must be handled separately. Example, not market evidence: 8h × ₹1,500 + ₹1,000 tools + ₹2,000 reserve = ₹15,000 modeled cost; ₹25,000 implies ₹10,000 or 40% modeled margin. If time grows to 12h with other costs held, ₹25,000 implies 16%; cut scope or reprice. Check delivery capacity and cash timing, not just margin.
- Proposal must say: buyer pain in their words, before/after deliverables, exact exclusions, staffed owners, data/tools, dependencies, price and payment milestones, acceptance and one next ask. No guarantees or customer claims without evidence.

### 3. Measure before expanding
Select 8–12 matched, scrubbed representative cases as a **feasibility** pilot, including wrong-version approval, missing claim evidence, duplicate approval, and ambiguous authority. Freeze the case set, baseline, criteria and size before running. Compare old and shadow flow on the same cases at equal quality, separately logging active human minutes (including retries, review, and rework), elapsed turnaround, first-pass acceptance count/denominator, and cost/case. If using different reviewers or run order, record the difference and don't imply a causal gain. A workable starter gate, to agree with the sponsor, is ≥20% improvement in median active minutes, first-pass quality no worse, and zero unauthorized downstream actions. A single violation or untested critical exception means hold; adopt only after an explicit go-live decision, staffed fallback and operator handoff.
- Approval is tied to role and exact artifact revision. New revision invalidates stale sign-off; a duplicate event never produces a second handoff. Creative approval is not permission to render, spend, publish or contact anyone.
- Use verified time savings only. Monthly break-even cases = monthly incremental cost ÷ (net measurable savings per case before monthly cost). If savings are zero/negative, no break-even. Scale only after an operator measures real use; never extrapolate a demo to an ROI promise.
- Keep one private weekly pipeline: buyer hypothesis → verified pain → proposed → paid → delivered → renewed. Track stage, evidence, estimated hours and next action; count no response as unknown, not demand. One 14-day test after outreach is approved: at most 10 qualified prospects, 3 discovery calls or 1 paid audit as an initial signal, ₹0 ad spend; if fewer than 2 meaningful replies, revise buyer/message before increasing volume. No outreach now.
