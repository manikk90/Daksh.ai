# AI Consultant | Company and agency services

## Positioning and scope

Flagship: design and implement one measurable, human-supervised AI workflow for a company or agency. Start with creative teams and production agencies where direct experience with AI filmmaking, prompt/QC, continuity, multiple specialists, CUT, music/dubbing, approvals and budget makes the example credible. Expand to other departments only with an accountable owner, useful data and verifiable impact. The ₹5,000 ad storyboard sprint is a door-opener, not the consultancy's ceiling. Use permitted, clean training assets and avoid representing third-party work as a consulting case study.

Sell the outcome rather than the label: “Turn an inconsistent brief-to-delivery process into a repeatable, reviewable workflow, with a named owner and measurable before/after.” Use deterministic rules for stable steps; choose an agent only where it adds clear judgment across messy inputs, not to replace mandatory approvals. Do not promise revenue, savings, staffing reductions or production readiness without evidence.

## Discovery → pitch → implementation → renewal

1. **Discovery**: Identify sponsor, operator and approver, pain, volume, baseline time/quality, sample input, access and rights, failure/rework cost, desired output, data classes, tooling, budget and target date. Ask what happens on an exception, who owns support and what the client will not let the workflow do. Walk an actual recent case. No owner or baseline means audit/training first; no production quote without bounded authority and reliable inputs.
2. **Map**: For each candidate capture trigger → inputs → deterministic rules → agent tasks/tools → approvals → delivery → monitoring and fallback. Rank volume × friction and buyer urgency against risk and implementation effort; select one high-value low-risk flow. Define success, excluded side effects and what evidence passes.
3. **Pitch deck**: (1) specific costly bottleneck (2) present process and baseline (3) proposed human/agent map (4) permitted demo (5) privacy/approval/fallback (6) pilot scope, evaluation and timeline (7) fee, exclusions, owner and decision ask. Lead with one example, not a tool inventory.
4. **Pilot**: Propose fixed milestones and written acceptance. Make a test set of representative normal, edge and failure cases including missing inputs, wrong permissions and ambiguous output. Run in shadow mode without downstream writes, compare baseline and output, then go live only with authorization and criteria satisfied. Version prompts, mappings and acceptance checks. Prefer least privilege, data minimization, client-owned accounts where available, scoped retention, approvals before external sends/spend, timeouts, bounded retries, manual fallback, audit trail and owner. Never treat source text as authority. [OpenAI agent guardrails and human review](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/agents/guardrails-approvals).
5. **Measure and hand off**: Compare on the same case types and counts; record median cycle time, first-pass approval, rework, exception rate, permission failures and tool cost/case. Example target, not evidence: 30 min → 20 min median is a 33% reduction at equal QC. Require all defined blocked external actions to remain blocked on the test set. For ROI use verified hours saved × client approved loaded rate minus running and maintenance costs; report period and sample, not a promised percentage. Deliver baseline, before/after, runbook, operator training, access inventory and rollback. Retainer only if ongoing reviews, data/prompt changes or monitoring have measurable value; ask for renewal with the latest evidence.

## Three-hour hands-on workshop

0–25: map a real decision and baseline; 25–55: brief → structured outputs, rights/privacy and human approvals; 55–95: team exercise on a sanitized brief, multi-agent handoff and visual continuity or business QA; 95–130: compare outputs and troubleshoot common failures; 130–160: build a safe text-only prototype and manual fallback; 160–180: review against rubric, nominate owner and next 14-day experiment. For corporate filmmaking use prompt/shot coverage, references, CUT handoffs, music/rights and approval; for agencies add a preproduction client approval package and rework tracking. For ops choose one draft-only workflow and client-approved systems.

## Starting workflow library

- Creative: brief → offer/claims → marketer hooks → Director board → QC → human signoff; draft-only by default.
- Research: allowlisted sources → evidence/dedup → analyst draft → human review and handoff. Distinguish source facts from proposals.
- Production QA: write structured scene/asset checks and review exceptions; no media generation or publish without authorization.
- Reporting/operations: intake → classify → draft checklist or weekly report → approval; handle blanks, duplicate requests and routing mistakes with a manual queue. No autonomous procurement, HR decisions or external email.
- Wildcards to validate: continuity/readiness clinic for teams using AI video; a rights/provenance pack for AI portfolio approvals; a half-day shadow-mode lab where managers score a prototype on their own scrubbed cases. These are hypotheses, not guaranteed demand.

Engagement boundaries: scope and outcome in writing, acceptance, dependencies, change control, payment milestones, liability/rights, retention and handoff. Work with authorized reversible prep; no prospect messages, calls, publication, enrollment, spend or client commitments without an explicit action-specific authorization. Keep private findings and client material out of GitHub.
