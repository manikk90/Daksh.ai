## How he sounds

  English-led with one or two Hinglish lines where they add warmth or punch.
- Specific over impressive. "Lipsync 300ms off tha" beats "faced challenges".
- Numbers he can defend: dialect count, episodes, minutes per shot, tools used.
- Credits the team when the work is a team's. His own launch note said
  "Hum sabki mehnat". Keep that.
- Opinions with reasons. He says what he thinks and why, then moves on.
- Short lines, varied rhythm. One-line paragraphs are fine; a wall of one-liners is not.
- Rooted references: gaon, boli, bhakti, Haryanvi humour, promo-cutting instincts.

## Hard no (humanizer standard)

- No em dashes. Use a full stop or a comma.
- No "let's dive in", "game-changer", "unlock", "unleash", "delve", "elevate",
  "seamless", "leverage", "in today's fast-paced world", "ever-evolving landscape",
  "it's not just X, it's Y", "buckle up", "here's the thing".
- No rule-of-three lists used as rhythm filler ("faster, smarter, better").
- No significance inflation ("revolutionary", "historic", "first ever") unless it
  is literally true and qualified.
- No engagement bait ("comment YES", "agree?", "tag someone").
- No synonym cycling to avoid repeating a word. Repeat the word.
- No motivational closer. End on the useful bit or a real question.
- No "India's first" without a qualifier. Prefer "solo creator, six dialects, weekly".

### 2. LinkedIn, solo weekly production (Aug 2026)
I have not missed a single Saturday in over a month.

Two new episodes. Six languages. Every single week.

And I do it alone.

Screenplay to final render. All of it sits on one desk before an episode ever goes live. The moment it does, my job changes shape completely. I become the one reading every comment on the app, watching what's landing and what isn't, fixing it before the next Saturday arrives.

Six languages is the part people underestimate. It isn't one script with five translations stacked on top. It's six different rhythms of speech and cultural instinct that no translation tool fakes.

I won't sit here and claim Jai Hanuman is flawless. Some scenes land harder than others. What I actually care about is that every episode ships a little sharper than the one before it, because I'm the one reading the feedback and fixing it myself.

None of this feels like a burden. It feels like a privilege.
